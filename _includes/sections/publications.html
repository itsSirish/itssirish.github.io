NeurIPS 2026 Main Track CORE A* Co-first author

Geometry-Aware Self-Supervised Electrophysiology Representation Learning

Can the electrical signal alone tell you where an electrode sits in the brain, without histology? We learn geometry-aware representations of raw single-channel LFP by using the probe's physical channel layout as a self-supervisory signal. The approach beats masked-prediction baselines, with gains that grow at larger data scales, and sets the state of the art for brain-region classification and 3D coordinate regression across datasets, labs, species, and probe technologies.

LFP2Gene: Recovering Local Molecular Composition from Local Field Potentials

LFPs are generated by the tissue right around the electrode, so do self-supervised LFP representations remember their molecular surroundings? Bridging IBL Neuropixels channels to MERFISH atlases through the Allen Common Coordinate Framework, a frozen encoder recovers a good deal of local gene expression on 34 held-out subjects, more than any alternative under the same protocol. The shared structure is real, low-dimensional (PLS and CCA agree on five dimensions), and organised at the scale of brain regions.

OJAMI: Electro-Optic Jitter Accelerated Multiphoton Imaging

S. Tucker, T. Reizis, E. Guralnik, D. Theagene, K. S. Guillory, V. S. Parupudi, D. Rinberg, F. St-Pierre, E. Varol & S. Shoham

An inexpensive add-on electro-optic scan module that resonates along a standard two-photon microscope's slow axis, giving an order-of-magnitude speed-up while keeping subcellular resolution, enough to record membrane voltage at 440 Hz. I worked on image reconstruction: mapping each laser pulse's scattered sample onto a regular grid with an adaptive-bandwidth Gaussian kernel (see voltage imaging below).

A Smartphone-Enabled Deep Learning Approach for Myocardial Infarction Detection Using ECG Traces for IoT-Based Healthcare Applications

A majority-voting ensemble of five quantized (FP16/INT8) transfer-learning models for real-time myocardial infarction detection on smartphones, reaching 99.34% accuracy under five-fold cross-validation.
